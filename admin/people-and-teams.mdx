---
title: "People and teams"
description: "Use Enterprise workspace controls to manage people, roles, teams, and lifecycle."
---

People and Teams are part of Enterprise company-wide administration. Free and Pro owners open **Settings**, then under **Workspace essentials** choose **Manage workspace** to invite people and manage ownership (backup download lives under **Advanced** on that page). Enterprise adds a **People** page with roles, teams, directory policy, scoped access, and lifecycle controls.

## Roles

Every member has one of three roles:

| Role   | Can do                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Admin  | Approve apps, manage people and teams, set guardrails and policy, and see the audit trail. The first person in a workspace is an admin (sometimes called the owner in product copy). |
| Member | Connect their agents and use the apps their passes permit.                                                                                                                           |
| Agent  | A non-human identity for automation, authenticated by an agent key rather than a browser session.                                                                                    |

## Add people

Add teammates on the People page as admins or members, and Passport emails each an invite. Invited people sit at the top of the list until they accept, with **Resend** beside each one and **Revoke invite** in its menu. You can also copy your workspace sign-in link and share it directly. A person's team is stored as their department; assign it when you add them or change it later. Activity export filters use the query parameter `department` for that team name.

**Needs attention**, at the top of the list, shows join requests to review and anyone who joined more than a day ago but hasn't connected an agent, with their sign-in link one click away. App requests stay in **Approvals**.

Once the workspace has ten or more people, search the list and filter it by team, role, or status (Active, Invited, or Deactivated). The list shows active and invited people by default. Each row's menu changes the person's team or role, copies the sign-in link, signs them out everywhere, or deactivates them. Each of those asks first.

Open a person for their own page. **Overview** shows how far their setup got and what comes next, their job title, team, and role (each saved as soon as you change it), their connected apps and agents, and **Security and account** actions. Their **Activity** tab is the audit trail for that person, with its own CSV export.

## Manage teams

Teams live alongside People. Each team row shows how many people it has, how many apps it can use, the team setup that reaches it, and its approval rules. Open a team to add or remove people, review what the team gets on **Access** (each part links to where it's edited), and see its published setups on **Setup**. The team's menu views its activity, renames it, or deletes it. Renaming a team carries its access rules forward. Deleting one asks where its people should move before removing the team's shared rules.

## Who can join

Free and Pro workspaces stay invite-only: only invited members can sign in. Domain join and domain discovery are **Enterprise** controls under workspace Settings.

On Enterprise, joining defaults to invite-only. You can instead allow anyone who verifies an email on a company domain to join as a member (a Slack-style domain policy), and optionally make the workspace discoverable to teammates on those domains. You control this and the list of allowed domains in Settings.

<Note>
  Consumer and disposable email domains never trigger domain join or domain discovery, so shared providers like
  gmail.com can never be used to walk into your workspace.
</Note>

## Deactivate and reactivate

Deactivating a member immediately signs them out everywhere and revokes their connected-account grants, so nothing sits at rest or re-arms later. Reactivating restores access; they reconnect their own accounts. Sign-in attempts by a deactivated member are recorded in the audit trail as a security signal.

<Note>
  The same lifecycle runs through SCIM. Deprovisioning a user in your IdP does exactly this, and Passport refuses to
  remove the last active admin. See [SCIM](/enterprise/scim).
</Note>

## Automation credentials

Connect Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and other supported agents from **Agents**. There is no separate managed-agent identity setup.

For scripts and CI, owners and admins open **Agents → Automations** on every plan. A credential authenticates Passport CLI with its own access, or with access narrowed by a delegated person's permissions. It is shown once, can be rotated in place, and can be revoked from the same dialog. A credential belongs to the person who created it: their Safety choices govern its agent, and deactivating them revokes it. See [Agent keys, their owners, and rotation](/admin/agents-and-access#agent-keys-their-owners-and-rotation).

Passport CLI accepts the credential through `PASSPORT_AGENT_KEY` and the workspace endpoint through `PASSPORT_ENDPOINT`. Store these in your CI secret store. This mode works without interactive sign-in and writes no local credential file. See [Passport CLI](/quickstart/cli).
