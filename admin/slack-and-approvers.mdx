Autopilot asks a person only when a rule says so. These features decide who that person is, where they answer, and how the rules improve over time.

## Approvals in Slack

An admin adds the Passport app to Slack from **Settings › Notifications** with **Add to Slack**. After that:

- Each approval request arrives as a direct message to the person who can answer it, with the action, its environment and class, the agent, the computer and folder, the command with secrets removed, and why Passport asked.
- **Approve once**, **For this session**, and **Decline** work right in Slack. Decline asks for an optional note the agent sees.
- Admins can also post Production requests to a channel. Anyone can see them there; only people allowed to approve can answer.
- When a request is decided anywhere (Passport, a phone notification, Slack, or a rule change), every Slack message for it updates in place, for example "Approved once by Maya in Slack".

Passport finds each person in Slack by their Passport email. If Slack has no account with that email, Settings says so and requests still arrive in Passport. Each person can turn Slack messages off for themselves.

If the app is removed from Slack or its access is revoked, Settings shows "Slack stopped working" and an admin can reconnect it. Self-hosted Passports need the Slack app configured by whoever runs them; until then Settings shows "Slack isn't set up on this Passport".

## Approval routing

On **Rules › Team**, **Approval routing** (Pro and up) decides who gets each team's approval requests. A route is one team (or everyone) in one environment, such as Support in Production:

- **Requests**: deletes and irreversible actions, or changes too.
- **Send to**: one **Person**, a **Group** where any one of them can decide (people, or anyone on a team), **On call**, or the workspace **Admins**.
- **On call** is a simple rotation: a list of people who take turns, handing off weekly on a day and time you choose, in your time zone. Changing the list keeps whoever is on call now on call. **Someone else covers this week** hands the rest of the week to another person; the rotation picks up again at the next handoff.
- **If nobody answers**: after 15 minutes to 4 hours, the request also goes to every admin. **Only before it expires** keeps the long wait: a reminder after about 2 hours, and every admin about 4 hours before it expires.

A team's own route decides who gets its requests; without one, the route for everyone does. Routed people get a phone notification and, when Slack is connected, a direct message. The person whose agent asked sees who it's waiting on, like "Waiting on Maya, on call". Nobody is routed their own request: their turn passes to the next person on call, and a route that would reach only the requester goes to the admins.

Each step is in the audit trail (**Settings › Audit**): where Passport sent the request and why, each reminder, and the escalation to admins.

### Team review of Production deletes

Without a route, once a workspace has a second person, a Delete or Irreversible action in Production (confirmed, or not placed yet and treated as Production) also goes to the workspace admins. With a route, it goes to the routed people instead. Either way the requester can still answer it themselves unless the route needs someone else; the first answer counts. Notifications, Slack, and Approvals show who else has it.

## Someone else must approve

A Production route can say **Production deletes need someone else to approve** (or changes and deletes, when it covers changes). Workspaces that set Production approvers before routing keep them: each became a Production route with the same people and the same rule.

- The person whose agent asked can't approve their own request, in Passport, Slack, a notification, or their agent's own prompt.
- Any one of the routed people is enough. The request shows who it needs, like "Needs Maya or Sam" or "Needs you, on call".
- The rule from the route for everyone applies to every team, even one with its own route.
- Nothing these settings cover runs on its own in Production, whatever the presets or exceptions say. Blocks still block.
- Undoing a covered Production action needs an approver too, even when it starts from Undo in Passport.
- When Passport only guessed where a request runs, its requester can say it's Production but not anything looser. An approver who's an admin can place it from the request's **This is:** line.

## Asking someone to review

From one of your work items in **Sessions**, **Ask for review** sends it to a teammate with an optional short note. It shows up in their Home under **Needs attention**, linking to the work, and they answer **Looks good** or with a short comment. Their answer shows up in your **Needs attention** until you dismiss it. The teammate can open the sessions you sent for a month. It's a quick second look, not a code review.

## Exception requests

When a team or workspace rule blocks something a member needs, they can **Request an exception** from the agent's message, a blocked row in a session, or the rule on the Rules page. They choose whether it should run or ask them first, for an hour, a day, or permanently, and say why.

Admins answer in **Approvals**, where the list can be filtered to **Rule requests**: approve (optionally changing how long) or decline with a note. Approving adds a rule just for that person that lifts the one rule for that one action. Team floors and Production approvers still apply. The member gets an email either way.

## Rule suggestions

When someone approves the same action in the same environment three times in two weeks, Passport offers to make it an exception just for them: "You approved railway redeploy in Staging 6 times this week. Always run it here?" Production deletes, irreversible actions, and anything a team floor keeps asking about are never suggested. An accepted suggestion is an ordinary exception, recorded in Activity as **Rule added** with "From an approval suggestion". See [Learning from your approvals](/member/safety#learning-from-your-approvals).

## Weekly summary

Every Monday, people who had agent activity get a short email: what Passport handled for them, what they approved, what was blocked, and any suggestions. Admins get the workspace version with totals, new environments, and requests waiting for them. Turn it off in **Settings › Notifications**.
