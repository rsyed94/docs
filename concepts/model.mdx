Passport sits between an agent and the work app it wants to use. It keeps the app credential, checks access and Autopilot rules, makes the call, and records the outcome.

## The five things to know

### Workspace

A workspace is your team's private space in Passport. It holds people, apps, settings, and Activity. Workspaces are isolated from one another, and you can switch if you belong to more than one.

### Apps

An app is something your agent can use through Passport, such as GitHub, Linear, Stripe, or Notion. Open **Apps** to browse, connect, and manage them.

Some apps connect through MCP. Others use a reviewed API integration. Passport gives both the same connection, access, rules, and Activity experience, so most people do not need to care which technology is underneath.

An app is also its vendor's CLI. Railway's tools, the `railway` commands agents run through `passport exec`, and the Railway key you keep in Passport all belong to the Railway app: its **Tools** tab lists the commands too, its **Keys** tab holds your key, its **Activity** tab includes the CLI, and its **Access** decides who may run it. AWS is a CLI app: it has no tools, just the AWS CLI with each person's key.

### Agents

An agent is where you use AI: Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, VS Code, Codex, Passport CLI, or another MCP-compatible product. Open **Agents** to add one, see its connection status, disconnect it, or choose which available apps it can see. On Pro and Enterprise, admins switch **Agents** to **Everyone** to see every agent in the workspace. On Enterprise, **Agents → Discovered** lists connections that scans found outside Passport.

### Access

Access answers one question: what may this person do with this app?

  <Card title="Full" icon="unlock">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Read-only" icon="glasses">
    Only Read tools. The rest are hidden from the agent.
  </Card>
  <Card title="None" icon="ban">
    The app is not shown to that person.

Pro and Enterprise admins can set access for the workspace, a team, or a person. A more specific choice wins. A person's agent-level app selection can narrow access further, but can never grant more.

### Activity

Every call through Passport creates an Activity record with the person, agent, app, action, outcome, and timing. Everyone sees their own records in **Sessions → All activity**. Pro and Enterprise admins switch it to **Everyone** for the workspace-wide log.

## What happens during a call

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the person and agent">
    Passport checks the signed-in identity and the connection being used.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply access, then rules">
    Access comes first: apps and tools the person may not use are hidden and blocked, and no rule can change that.
    Then Autopilot exceptions, team floors, and presets run, block, or hold the action for approval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect the request">Data checks inspect outgoing arguments before Passport contacts the app.</Step>
  <Step title="Call the app">Passport uses the connected account on the person's behalf.</Step>
  <Step title="Inspect and record the result">
    Data checks inspect the result, then Passport writes Activity and returns the safe response.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## People, teams, and automation

- **Admins** manage the workspace and its rules.
- **Members** connect agents and use the apps available to them.
- **Agents** are the AI tools connected to Passport, such as Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor.
- **Automation credentials** connect scripts and CI through Passport CLI.
- **Teams** let Pro and Enterprise admins apply access and team setups to a group instead of one person at a time.

Deactivating a person revokes their sessions, agents, and connected accounts immediately.

## Advanced building blocks

- A [bundle](/admin/bundles) combines a curated set of tools from several apps behind one connection.
- [Tool discovery modes](/concepts/discovery) control how a large tool catalog is presented to an agent.
- [Managed API integrations](/admin/managed-api-integrations) connect reviewed provider APIs through the same Passport gateway.

<Accordion title="Technical connection URLs">
  | URL | Purpose |
  | --- | --- |
  | `/mcp` | Whole workspace, for every agent and agent token. Sign-in picks the workspace. Add `?ws={workspace}` or `?enroll={key}` to pre-select one. |
  | `/w/{workspace}/mcp` | An older address for the same thing. It keeps working; `passport init` moves agents to `/mcp`. |
  | `/b/{bundleKey}/mcp` | One bundle, for setups installed from **Apps › Setups** |

Add `https://passportmcp.com/mcp`. It connects to your workspace when you sign in, and an agent token names its workspace itself, so `PASSPORT_ENDPOINT` is the same URL. See [Passport CLI](/quickstart/cli). Pick which apps each agent sees on its page in **Agents**.

</Accordion>
