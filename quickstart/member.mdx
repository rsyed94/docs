title: "Get connected"
description: "Connect a work app, add your AI client, and confirm your first Passport action."
---

This is the shortest path from sign-in to a working AI client. It works on Windows, Linux, and macOS. You do not need the Passport desktop app.

## 1. Sign in

Open your invite, or sign in at [passportmcp.com](https://passportmcp.com). If your company uses single sign-on, Passport will send you to your company's sign-in page.

After signing in, you should see **Apps**, **Clients**, **Activity**, and **Settings**.

## 2. Connect a work app

Open **Apps**.

- Choose an app you already have access to and select **Connect**.
- Complete the provider sign-in in the Passport Connect window.
- If your workspace requires approval or company setup, Passport shows that instead of pretending the app is ready.

Your provider credentials stay in Passport. They are not copied into Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, Codex, or another AI client.

## When your agent asks you to connect again

If an app was never connected—or its sign-in expired—Passport can now let your AI agent show the next step right where you are working.

- Some AI clients show a built-in **Connect** or **Reconnect** action.
- Other clients show a secure Passport link in the conversation.
- Open the link, confirm that it uses your Passport domain, and finish the provider sign-in in your browser.
- Return to the agent when Passport says **All set**. The agent can retry the original task; it never receives your provider password or token.

The link is short-lived and tied to your Passport account. Another signed-in person cannot use it to connect their account on your behalf.

## 3. Add your AI client

Open **Clients**, choose **Add client**, then pick where you want to use Passport.

Passport shows current instructions for each client:

- **Cursor and VS Code** have one-click setup.
- **Claude Code and Codex** use their own MCP command and browser sign-in.
- **Claude Desktop, Claude.ai, and ChatGPT** use the connector address Passport gives you.
- **Terminal and automation** can use [Passport CLI](/quickstart/cli).
- **Other clients** can copy the advanced connector address if they support remote MCP and browser sign-in.

Follow the steps in Passport and finish the browser sign-in. No provider token should be pasted into your AI client.

<Note>
  On macOS, Passport Desktop is optional. It can set up several local clients and help discover direct MCP connections. For a supported direct connection, Desktop previews the change before you choose **Route through Passport** or **Remove**; it backs up and verifies a completed removal. **Desktop compatibility** is also available when a client's normal browser sign-in cannot reach a localhost, private, or self-hosted Passport server.
</Note>

## 4. Confirm the connection

Keep the AI client open and ask:

> List my available Passport tools.

Return to **Clients**. Passport marks the client connected after it sees real activity. Then choose **Run setup check** in the checklist. The check is safe, does not contact the app provider, and opens **Activity** so you can see the result.

If setup stays pending, open that client in **Clients** and use **Troubleshoot**. See [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting) for common fixes.

## Make it yours

- Open a connected client to choose which of your available apps it can see.
- Open [Safety preferences](/member/safety) to ask before write or destructive actions.
- Open **Activity** to review what your clients did and whether Passport allowed, blocked, or failed the action.
