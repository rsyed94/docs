Passport is the place your team connects work apps to AI assistants. You connect GitHub, Linear, Stripe, and other tools once. Then Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, and the rest use them through Passport, with your company's rules and a clear Activity log.

You do not need to understand protocols to get started. Connect an app, add an AI assistant, and check that it worked.

Free and Pro focus on getting people connected. Enterprise adds company-wide controls for people, approvals, clients, and audit export — without changing the everyday setup flow.

## Get started in three steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect an app">
    Open **Apps**, pick something you use (for example GitHub), and choose **Connect**. See the [workspace owner quickstart](/quickstart/admin) if you are setting up the workspace.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add an AI assistant">
    Open **AI Clients → Add client**. Follow the steps for Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT, or another supported client. On Windows, Linux, and macOS the browser path works the same; the macOS desktop app is optional. See the [member quickstart](/quickstart/member).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that everything works">
    On **Home** or **AI Clients**, choose **Run setup check**. Passport runs a safe test and opens **Activity** when it succeeds, so you can see the result.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Accordion title="A note on terminology">
  In the product, **Apps** is where you browse and connect tools. Those tools often speak **MCP** (Model Context Protocol) under the hood — that is the technical name for the connection. These docs lead with everyday language and introduce MCP where it helps. The first person in a workspace is an **admin** (sometimes called the owner); Free and Pro keep admin work under **Manage workspace**, while Enterprise adds fuller **Workspace controls**.
</Accordion>
